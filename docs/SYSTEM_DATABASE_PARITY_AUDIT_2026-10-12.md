The audit covers the documented alterations and approved workflows in the conversation, engineering record, recovery manifest, study guides and release reports. ## Reproducible findings

### PARITY-01 — Older saved rice evidence is stale (P1: member meal availability)

**Evidence:** Read-only inspection of 14 nonsuperseded unfinished cycles finds 76 stale cooked-rice component records across six cycles. These cycles include past dates: **58 records are past-date; 18 are today/future across two cycles**. Only **three** pass the strict unchanged unrestricted-serving maintenance guard. The other 15 future records do not qualify for that guard and require individual current-context assessment. These are saved evidence revisions, not missing catalogue nutrient values.

**Reproduce:** On an isolated recovery copy, invoke the normal complete-plate clearance path for a saved cycle with cooked-rice revision zero against FNRI revision one. The slot remains withheld despite its persisted recipe approval. A normal read must not rewrite evidence. The known repaired cycle, checked separately, remains **21 cleared meals, three per day**, with no stale rice component.

**Limits:** Full clearance was computed for the six affected cycles and the repaired target, not every unaffected cycle. Three cycles have fewer saved rows than expected (20/21, 6/18, 0/21); the last has `SAFETY_REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`. Their dates/context and legitimate safety exclusions must be inspected before calling missing slots a transfer defect. Job inventory is 18 completed and two failed (`GENERATION_FAILED`); no new job retry was performed.

**Next action:** Back up and preview an explicit target list; repair only individually eligible unchanged general servings. Assess other current/future slots through existing safety/review workflows. Preserve past history, nutrients, identities, decisions and logs. Do not bulk refresh every stale record or bypass safety gates.

### PARITY-02 — Clean-checkout CSV imports fail on line endings (P2: recovery reproducibility)

**Evidence:** Repository policy pins the original FNRI raw delivery hash `669a0dc88284f99719e6a2f3e091fbbc9653447da03edf73e012215c71fc7f24` (265,325 bytes, CRLF). Git's `text=auto eol=lf` produces 263,783 bytes with hash `ccddc6103dbd1ef91bd76ad39a4f139d3b1119660bd5c5f4e823b6df5c9479f9`. The importer checks raw bytes before parsing and throws “FNRI source fingerprint changed. Review new delivery first.”

**Reproduce:** Run nutrient-completion/demo-preparation acceptance from a clean LF checkout. It rejects the fixture before applying changes. Repeating on the task-owned fixture with the original pinned CSV passes 12 completion groups and 14 preparation groups. Application code was unchanged between these runs; only the verified original delivery and local fixture wiring differed.

**Impact:** Existing live catalogue rows remain intact. Future reproducible restores/preparation/import checks fail on a harmless transport conversion.

**Next action:** Define a canonical content fingerprint that accepts only line-ending transport normalization, retaining the original delivery hash as provenance. Add CRLF/LF equivalence and substantive-change rejection regressions. Do not disable fingerprint validation or silently accept an arbitrary new dataset.
